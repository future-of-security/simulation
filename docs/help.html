Help & Rules

Everything you need to know to play the simulation

How the Simulation Works

You are part of a crisis management exercise. Each team plays a specific role in responding to a cascading crisis scenario set in Southwest Virginia.

  • Teams: 13 (3 students each)
  • Duration: 60 minutes per phase
  • Roles rotate each phase — your team gets a new role and starting conditions

Each Phase

  1. Check the dashboard for your team's role, budget, and trust level
  2. Review the active incidents visible to your role
  3. Coordinate with other teams if needed
  4. Submit your response report on Canvas
  5. The facilitator processes responses and updates the dashboard with new incidents and scores

Steps 2 through 5 repeat several times inside the class hour. Submit as often as you want; those in-class submissions are working drafts and none of them is your last.

Two Stages, One Deadline

The phase itself runs in class and ends when the facilitator closes it and starts the debrief. The assignment stays open until 11:59pm that night, and everything you submitted counts toward your grade. What the final submission has to carry is the two sections that look back on the whole phase, which you could not have written mid-class.

You never have to guess which submission is your final one: nothing you submit during class can be, because the deadline is hours after class ends. Two sections of the report exist only in that final version, since they look back on the phase as a whole.

The deadline is hard. Canvas locks at the same moment it comes due, so there is no submission after it and no late credit.

Submitting Your Response

Your team submits a Response Report on Canvas, as many times as you like during the phase and once more that night. Use this template:

Everything through Remaining Budget can be written during class. Outcomes, Needs, Risks and Reflection and Design Takeaway look back on the phase as a whole, so they belong in the report you file that evening — if your team has anything to say in them; a genuinely empty section earns nothing.

How your report is graded: submissions are read by an AI served from llm.arc.vt.edu, working against the phase rubric. It reads what you wrote, not how much. Before submitting, delete every bracket line — the placeholders and examples are instructions, not content — and omit any section you have nothing to report in (the final-only sections included). The shortest report that shows your team acted is the best one.

How the Report Is Graded

Each phase is worth 100 points, scored against a rubric attached to the phase's assignment on Canvas. Open the assignment and click Show Rubric: it lists every criterion, what section of your report it is read from, and what separates a strong answer from a thin one. That rubric is the only copy, so it is the one to check.

Which submission is graded

Most criteria are read across everything you submitted during the phase, not just the last version. A transfer you recorded at minute 20 still counts even if the final report does not repeat it, so you are not penalized for a final report that reads as a summary.

The reflection criterion is read from the last report you submit before the deadline, and only that one. Leaving that section blank scores at the bottom of the scale no matter what you wrote earlier in the phase or said out loud in the room.

Volume earns nothing. A long report that never names its own uncertainty scores below a short one that does, and reporting on incidents your role cannot see is not rewarded.

Actions & Budget

Using Catalog Actions

The Action Catalog lists all pre-approved actions your team can take. Each action has:

  • Available To: Which roles can use this action
  • Cost: Deducted from your budget
  • Delay: Time before the action takes effect (0 = immediate)
  • Requires Approval: Some actions need another team's sign-off
  • Trust Impact: How this action affects your public trust score

Custom Actions

You can propose actions not in the catalog. The facilitator will evaluate feasibility, adjust costs, and determine outcomes. Custom actions can be:

  • Approved — proceeds as proposed
  • Approved with modifications — cost or effect adjusted
  • Partially effective — helps but less than hoped
  • Denied — not feasible for your team
  • Backfires — makes things worse

Cost Splitting on Collaborative Actions

When multiple teams collaborate on the same catalog action targeting the same incident, they split the cost equally:

CollaborationCost per Team
2 teams50% each
3 teams33% each
4+ teamsEqual split

Requirements: Both teams must document the collaboration in their response reports for the split to apply.

No split applies when:

  • Teams take the same action independently on different incidents — each pays the full cost
  • Teams take different actions on the same incident — each pays their own action's full cost
  • Custom actions — costs are evaluated per proposal, not split

Budget Transfers

Teams can transfer budget to other teams to fund actions they can't take themselves. Both teams must document the transfer. The receiving team must still take the action — a transfer alone doesn't resolve anything.

Trust System

Trust represents public confidence in your organization. It starts at the value shown on the dashboard and changes based on your actions.

Trust LevelEffect
80–100%Actions cost 20% less, community cooperation, faster approvals
50–70%Normal effectiveness
30–40%Actions cost 25% more, community resistance, media scrutiny
10–20%Actions may fail (25% chance), protests, delayed responses

What Changes Trust

  • +10% to +20%: Successful actions, good communication, collaboration, creative solutions
  • -10% to -20%: Failed/misleading communication, ignoring critical incidents, backfired actions
  • -10%: Not submitting a response when you had severity 4-5 incidents
  • Trust changes are capped at +30% / -30% per update cycle

Scoring

Teams earn points by resolving incidents. There are no penalty points — you earn for successes, not punished for failures.

Escalated Incidents — Mitigation Credit

When an initial incident escalates because the deadline passed, it is marked Escalated and is no longer eligible for full points. However, teams that completed their portion of the response before the deadline still earn partial credit:

  • Each initial incident has 3 teams, each with an explicit response requirement (RESPONSE COUNTS AS)
  • A team that met its requirement before the deadline earns a proportional share of the incident's point value (equal split among all required teams)
  • A team that did not meet its requirement earns 0
  • The escalation incident that fires afterward is scored independently — resolving it is still worth full escalation points

Collaboration Split

When multiple teams collaborate to resolve an incident:

  • 2 teams: 60% of points each (rewards collaboration)
  • 3+ teams: 40% of points each

Collaboration

Many incidents require multiple teams to resolve. Collaboration is rewarded:

  • Both teams must document the collaboration in their response reports
  • Collaborating teams get +1 trust each
  • Point splits reward collaboration (60% each for 2 teams is better than 100% solo in most cases)
  • Some incidents can only be resolved through cross-team action

Intelligence Sharing

Each phase's action catalog includes an Intelligence Sharing action available to ALL teams at no cost. Sharing information with other teams builds trust and improves collective response. Check your phase's action catalog for the specific action ID.

Tips for Success

  • Read your incidents carefully — click each one on the dashboard for full details
  • Check which actions you can take — filter the action catalog by your role
  • Watch your budget — you can't spend more than you have
  • Coordinate early — reach out to teams whose roles complement yours
  • Propose custom actions — creative solutions are welcome and earn trust
  • Don't ignore critical incidents — severity 4-5 incidents that go unaddressed will escalate and cost you trust
  • Submit promptly — not submitting costs trust and lets incidents escalate
  • Track your spending — list remaining budget in your report
